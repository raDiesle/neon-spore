# Touch, choreography and co-op — what other games do

> **Status: not built, and none of it accepted.** A study, written on 26
> September 2026 because the owner asked for it: other games whose fights are
> choreographed and answered by touch, the co-op ones, every input a phone
> browser can read and which of them this game leaves unused, videos to go and
> watch, and boss sequences worth carrying over. An entry leaves this page the
> way [transfers](transfers.md) says: into [ideas](ideas.md) or a boss card,
> or into the refusals at the bottom.

**How far to trust it.** Everything here came from web searches. The pages
themselves — Wikipedia, the fan wikis, the GDC slides — were mostly refused by
the network the search ran behind, so a game's details are what the search
results said about it. **No video was watched.** Each link appeared in a
result; none was made up. A picture is YouTube's own thumbnail of the linked
video, so it shows whatever frame the uploader chose, not necessarily the
moment described. Anything the search could not confirm says *unverified*.

**What "choreographed" means here** is what it means in
[bosses-choreographed](bosses-choreographed.md): a boss that runs a written
scene — steps, each with a seat, a gesture, a target and a window in beats —
rather than a loop. So the question put to each game is always the same: how
does it *tell* you the next step, how long before it has to be answered, and
what does it do when you miss.

The five tests every entry is read against are
[transfers' filter](transfers.md#the-filter): it survives 0.5–2 s of voice;
nothing travels; it reads at 26 px in portrait; it asks the pair something new
about each other; it never needs to know what was said.

## 1. One player, a touch screen, a choreographed fight

The strongest fits first. Each ends with what survives the trip.

### Rhythm Heaven — Nintendo DS, 2008 (JP) / 2009

![Rhythm Heaven, Shoot-'Em-Up, a Perfect run](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/AUBC13cxu7o/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** Tap, flick, slide, and hold then release, anywhere on the screen.
Where you touch never matters; when you touch, and which gesture, is
everything. The DS is held on its side like a book — a portrait screen.

**How a fight is written.** Every minigame teaches one call-and-response rule
and then varies it. Fifty of them, in ten sets of four plus a Remix that splices
the four into one song without their tutorials.

- **Shoot-'Em-Up** is the closest thing on this page to Neon Spore: a fixed
  spaceship, aliens arriving in a group. *The order and spacing in which they
  appear is the tell.* Exactly four beats later they attack in the same order
  and rhythm, and each is shot on its beat. A miss damages the ship. It
  escalates by bigger groups, syncopation and tempo — never by a new input.
- **Moai Doo-Wop.** The left statue sings a phrase; yours repeats it. "Doo-wop"
  is a hold for as long as the *doo* lasted, released on the *wop* — the
  length of the hold is copied from the call.
- **The Remixes** bring back earlier rules without their tutorials and switch
  every few bars, the scenery change the only warning.

**What survives.** *Shown, then played back* — the boss plays its phrase, the
pair plays it back a fixed number of beats later. See [§3.1](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over).
The Remix is a final-exam boss made of earlier steps; see [§3.14](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over).

[Shoot-'Em-Up, Perfect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AUBC13cxu7o) ·
[Shoot-'Em-Up 2, Perfect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o876na5Vr7M) ·
[Moai Doo-Wop, Perfect](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_zFcN1kGdec) ·
[Remix 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aFtS0e7GYZA)

### Thumper: Pocket Edition — iOS, 2018 (Drool)

![Thumper, the level 1 boss](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/3fpxtDUcSgQ/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** Tap on a thump; hold to slide through barriers; swipe up to jump,
down to pound; on a turn, hold and slide sideways without lifting.

**How a fight is written.** The track is the countdown: everything comes toward
you along it. A boss sits at the end. A chain of green thumps leads to a pad
that fires at it — and **one miss turns the rest of the chain blue, and the
whole chain loops until it is played perfectly.** A boss takes four hits. The
recurring boss, Crakhed, is built from the level's earlier gimmicks: the exam
for that level.

**What survives.** *Only a clean phrase hurts, and a broken one comes round
again* ([§3.2](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)). The track itself does
not — the player rides it.

[Level 1 boss](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fpxtDUcSgQ) ·
[Level 1 final boss, S rank](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGX53p8b5JA) ·
[Pocket Edition page](https://thumpergame.com/pocket-edition/) ·
[the touch-lane update](https://toucharcade.com/2018/02/19/thumper-update/)

### Superbrothers: Sword & Sworcery EP — iOS, 2011 (Capybara)

![Sword & Sworcery, the first boss](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/rmsf7lCbfWk/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** In a fight, two: tap the sword, or tap the shield, on the beat.

**How a fight is written.** Enemies play the rhythm before it has to be
answered, by beating on their own shields. **Trigon** is three phases:

1. It fires beams. Strike each one with the sword when it is close. After
   enough, the beam turns red and comes faster.
2. A charging sound, and the player's shield glows. Tap the shield, quickly.
   The sound and the glow are the tell.
3. Both at once: deflect the triangles with the sword *and* tap the shield
   against separate blasts. The two answers interleave.

**What survives.** A clean three-movement template — teach answer A, teach
answer B, interleave them — and in a two-seat game the interleave is the
interesting part, because A is one phone and B is the other
([§3.3](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)).

[First boss](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rmsf7lCbfWk) ·
[Bright Moon Trigon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=okqgIVTCuGI) ·
[Dark Moon Trigon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Meesqld9d1s)

### Infinity Blade — iOS, 2010 (Chair / Epic)

![Infinity Blade, the God King](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/GZcdKttSA0g/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** Swipe to attack. **Parry**: swipe against the incoming strike, at
the moment it lands. **Dodge**: a button bottom-left or bottom-right. **Block**:
tap the shield, which wears out. **Magic**: draw a symbol — a circle is fire, a
lightning bolt is shock, a U heals.

**How a fight is written.** Every attack has a wind-up pose per direction; the
pose is the tell and the answer matches its direction. The God King mostly
swings blades but slips in **kicks that cannot be parried** and shield bashes
that break a light shield — *the same wind-up asking for a different answer* —
and his combos always end on a strike to the centre, a last beat you can learn.

**What survives.** *The exception that changes the answer*
([§3.4](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)). The dodge does not: it is a
sidestep, and nothing here travels.

[God King at level 100](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GZcdKttSA0g) ·
[intro, God King and ending](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MUkfX6Fc7zI) ·
[combat rules](https://infinityblade.fandom.com/wiki/Combat)

### The World Ends With You: Solo Remix — iOS, 2012 (Square Enix)

![TWEWY Solo Remix, Draco Cantus](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/jnlmK0VDIhA/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** Each "pin" is bound to one: tap an enemy, draw a circle in empty
space, press and hold empty space, slash across the hero, drag him into an
enemy, tap fast, draw lines. The partner character has gestures of their own.
(Some DS pins read the microphone — refused.)

**How a fight is written.**

- **Draco Cantus**, five heads. At every fifth of its health, a partner glows
  and strikes a pose; *the pose names the gesture*. Do that partner's gesture
  and a Fusion fires. **Miss it and the boss heals.**
- **Minamimoto**: touch him and he teleports and becomes invincible — except
  while he is attacking or recoiling. *Do not touch until he commits.*

**What survives.** A boss that names the gesture by showing who owns it
([§3.12](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)); a missed step that heals
rather than hurts ([§3.11](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)); and restraint as
a step ([§3.4](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)).

[Draco Cantus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jnlmK0VDIhA) ·
[Panthera Cantus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=beUtR04MEg8) ·
[the touch controls, reviewed](https://toucharcade.com/2012/08/28/the-world-ends-with-you-solo-remix-review/)

### Elite Beat Agents / Osu! Tatakae! Ouendan — DS, 2005–2006 (iNiS)

![Elite Beat Agents, the finale](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/2KJtGTAARxQ/hqdefault.jpg)

**Gestures.** Numbered circles tapped in order as a shrinking ring meets each
one; a ball followed along a drawn path, sometimes reversing at a U-turn; a
disc spun until its bars fill.

**How a fight is written.** Position and number are both choreography. The
shrinking ring is a countdown on every note. Each song is cut into sections,
and **at the end of each section the story branches** — an O panel if the meter
is healthy, an X if not — and the ending depends on the count.

**What survives.** A boss whose *next step* depends on how the last phrase went
([§3.11](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)). The shrinking ring is the
tell `BossSequenceStep` already needs a picture for.

[EBA finale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2KJtGTAARxQ) ·
[EBA, "The Finale"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BUyMKmINek4)

### The Legend of Zelda: Spirit Tracks — DS, 2009

![Spirit Tracks, Malladus](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/yz_jCdTG5W0/hqdefault.jpg)

**Malladus**, the last phases: slash away the fireballs aimed at Zelda; when the
cursor on him **turns yellow**, fire a light arrow; three arrows and he falls;
after the third, **rub the stylus back and forth** to finish. Three gestures in
order — slash, timed tap, rub. (The flute is blown into the microphone —
refused.)

**What survives.** *Three to open it, then the finisher*
([§3.5](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)), and the rub as a finishing
verb this game does not have ([§4.3](#43-missed-worth-considering)).

[Malladus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yz_jCdTG5W0)

### Magic Touch: Wizard for Hire — iOS/Android, 2015 (Nitrome)

![Magic Touch, the trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/iY9xmlTLw6o/hqdefault.jpg)

Knights drift down on balloons, each balloon wearing a symbol. **Draw a symbol
anywhere and every balloon wearing it pops.** The King Wizard boss spins down
wearing one complex symbol that has to be drawn several times running (five,
says one source — *unverified*) while small balloons keep coming. Not tied to a
beat; the pressure is the descent.

**What survives.** One drawing answering every matching body at once — read the
whole field before you draw. And a symbol one phone sees and the other draws
([§3.15](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)).

[Trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iY9xmlTLw6o) ·
[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/magic-touch-wizard-for-hire/id946917251) ·
[the update that added the boss](https://toucharcade.com/2015/04/15/magic-touch-wizard-for-hire-update/)

### Kirby: Canvas Curse — DS, 2005 (HAL)

![Kirby Canvas Curse, Paint Panic](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/k_zrdToBcdU/hqdefault.jpg)

In **Paint Panic** the boss draws a picture while a row of bombers counts
down; trace it before they go off. The boss draws the step and the player draws
it back.

[Paint Panic](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k_zrdToBcdU)

### Muse Dash — iOS/Android, 2018 (PeroPeroGames)

![Muse Dash, boss animations](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/IGYhhM1P63o/hqdefault.jpg)

Tap the left or right half of the screen for the air or ground lane; hold for
long notes, sometimes while the other thumb taps the other lane; mash for
special enemies. **The boss visibly fires the chart** — phase one throws small
bullets that are ordinary notes, phase two two kinds of bigger ones — so the
notes read as its choreography rather than as a scrolling list. A large **"!"**
over it warns of a charge, and the answer is a mash.

**What survives.** A boss as the *source* of what comes at the hull, which is
what a blob boss that throws parts of itself already is (THE SCUTTLE's
`SpentBody`). One thumb holding while the other taps is already the game's
(strip plus lobe).

[New boss animations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IGYhhM1P63o) ·
[Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.prpr.musedash)

### Sayonara Wild Hearts — Apple Arcade, 2019 (Simogo)

![Sayonara Wild Hearts, Stereo Lovers](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/Yyy5rHWkTus/hqdefault.jpg)

Each level is one pop song and each boss a dance. **Stereo Lovers**: a masked
swordsman splits into twins; you parry their swords, generously timed; the
swords grow until they smash buildings; then giant summoned swords answered
only by rhythm presses; then the swords merge into a plane. **The gesture never
changes; the thing it answers keeps getting bigger.** The steering does not
survive — it travels.

**What survives.** Escalation carried by the picture rather than by new inputs —
a look, and so VERSUS's business, not this page's
([§3.18](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)).

[Stereo Lovers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yyy5rHWkTus) ·
[Little Death](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oGhc-1rimz4)

### Rhythm Doctor — PC 2021, iOS as *Rhythm Doctor Mobile* (7th Beat Games)

![Rhythm Doctor on a phone](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/qZKhuun1dSo/hqdefault.jpg)

One button: hit the seventh beat of a patient's heartbeat. The rows are the
tell. Levels get harder by changing *how it reads*, never what you press: 1-XN
switches to 7/8 halfway; in 5-X the game's window itself moves and resizes.
Two-player mode gives each player one half of the keyboard.

[Gameplay on a phone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qZKhuun1dSo) ·
[App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rhythm-doctor-mobile/id6756513430)

### Chart games, as a dictionary of touches: Cytus II, Arcaea, Phigros, Lanota

![Lanota on an iPhone](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/EKfiPjQ8XSg/hqdefault.jpg)

Not boss fights, but the most worked-out touch vocabularies there are.

- **Cytus II** — a scan line sweeping up and down, changing speed mid-song;
  tap, hold, long hold, drag chains, flick in any direction.
  [Note types](https://cytus.fandom.com/wiki/Notes)
- **Arcaea** — four floor lanes plus 3D arcs traced in the air; a "hard gauge"
  survival mode used for its boss-like charts.
  [Grievous Lady](https://arcaea.fandom.com/wiki/Grievous_Lady)
- **Phigros** — judgment *lines* that move, turn, multiply and fade: the stage
  is choreographed as well as the notes. Its *Drag* note only needs a finger
  present on the line — presence, not a moment.
  [Mechanics and windows](https://phigros.fandom.com/wiki/Game_Mechanics)
- **Lanota** — notes travel outward from the centre of a disc to its rim and
  are answered **where on the rim they land**; the disc itself turns and
  resizes. A round hull with a cannon sliding along it is exactly this shape.
  [iPhone gameplay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EKfiPjQ8XSg) ·
  [review](https://toucharcade.com/2016/08/02/lanota-review/)
- **Beatstar** — hold then swipe as one note: a held note ending in a
  direction. [Play Store](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details/Beatstar+-+Touch+Your+Music?id=com.spaceapegames.beatstar&hl=en&gl=US)

### Without a touch screen, but with the best tells

- **Hi-Fi Rush** (2023) — before an attack the enemy glows, pulses, and *plays
  its attack rhythm* in light and sound, a rhythm that need not match the
  level's beat; then it attacks with exactly that timing and each hit is
  parried. Kale ends verses with a combo holding one hit that must not be
  parried. [Kale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nltY0LVBoWI) ·
  [Kale, S rank](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q90Eh-GfyJU)
- **Punch-Out!!** (Wii, 2009) — **Great Tiger's turban jewel flashes a colour
  per attack**: red a left jab, blue a left uppercut, green a right uppercut.
  In his combo it flashes *the whole sequence in order* before he throws it.
  Glass Joe's taunt is a tell that also opens a window to punish.
  [every Great Tiger animation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_J24oOdXgU) ·
  [challenges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GpD2D85wqvk)
- **Patapon** (PSP, 2007) — commands are four-beat drum phrases; Dodonga rears
  back before fire and dips its head before a headbutt, and *the pose chooses
  which phrase answers it*. [Dodonga](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8DtNzte9fk)
- **Just Shapes & Beats** (2018) — every hazard appears first as a translucent
  outline or a dotted edge, pink areas brighten as the hit nears, a "dance
  floor" flashes before it turns lethal. The dodging does not transfer; the
  warning vocabulary does. [final boss](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nOLNS--rl4) ·
  [every boss, hardcore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1jPOfZdNdCk)
- **Friday Night Funkin'** (browser, 2020) — the opponent sings a pattern of
  arrows, greyed on their side, and the player mirrors it. The plain
  call-and-response template, and it runs in a browser. No link captured.

### Weaker fits, for completeness

A Dance of Fire and Ice (one tap per tile; each world ends on a "boss" level of
the same rule), Crypt of the NecroDancer Pocket Edition and Lost in Harmony
(every move travels), Implosion and Shadow Fight 2 (action, not choreography),
Okamiden (Orochi's heads brought down by a brush stroke that pours sake into
three mouths; a boss that drains the player's ink — attacking the input
itself), Hogwarts Mystery (duels are wand shapes traced from the screen),
Fruit Ninja (the pomegranate: a finisher mashed by swiping), Groove Coaster
(hidden "ad-lib" notes), Kid Icarus Uprising (aiming, not choreography).
[Orochi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a3skohD1e1I) ·
[Emperon, Implosion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rJvmjq7Yb1U) ·
[ADOFAI](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fizzd.connectedworlds&hl=en_US)

## 2. Two players, and what makes them talk

Ordered from the nearest to this game's shape — two devices, the information
split, voice as the only channel — outward.

### Tick Tock: A Tale for Two — phones and PC, 2019 (Other Tales)

![Tick Tock, the trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/EOyIaA_Zlvc/hqdefault.jpg)

Two copies on two devices, same room or a voice call. Half of every answer is on
each screen; the same building can be furnished differently on the two. In the
**symbol machine**, each player is given symbols in order, calls theirs out, and
*both* must press every symbol either of them named, fast.

**What survives.** A step where each phone is both caller and answerer
([§3.10](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)); the same body drawn two ways
(`PerSeatTruth`).

[Steam, with screenshots](https://store.steampowered.com/app/790740/Tick_Tock_A_Tale_for_Two/) ·
[trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EOyIaA_Zlvc) ·
[walkthrough with the symbol machine](https://www.appunwrapper.com/2019/03/10/tick-tock-a-tale-for-two-walkthrough-guide/)

### The Past Within — phones and PC, 2022 (Rusty Lake)

![The Past Within, the trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/vddlEmrbNRw/hqdefault.jpg)

One player in the past, seeing flat hand-drawn scenes; the other in the future,
turning a 3D cube. **The same object in two projections.** Printouts in the
world give orders to *the other* player: "Person in The Future places black cube
in C-9." Not timed.

[Official page](https://www.rustylake.com/adventure-games/the-past-within.html) ·
[trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vddlEmrbNRw)

### Spaceteam — phones, 2012 (Henry Smith)

![Spaceteam, the trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/y3fsvKnIVJg/hqdefault.jpg)

Already the named reference ([transfers](transfers.md)); listed for two
details. "ASTEROID! (everybody shake)" appears on every screen and stays until
all shake together; a wormhole turns everyone's device upside down. Here that
is a window of a beat or more, drawn on both phones — never "at the same
millisecond".

[Trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y3fsvKnIVJg) ·

### Operation: Tango — PC and consoles, 2021 (Clever Plays)

![Operation Tango, the preview trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/ldTPI46tico/hqdefault.jpg)

The Agent sees the physical world, the Hacker the digital one. A code only the
Hacker sees, only the Agent can enter. And **crossed panels**: both operate a
complicated panel at once, and each panel's instructions are on the *other*
screen. The final mission's bomb stage is said to ask both to time actions and
match pictures — *unverified*.

[Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1335790/Operation_Tango/) ·
[preview trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ldTPI46tico) ·

### We Were Here Together — PC and consoles (Total Mayhem)

![We Were Here Together, the release trailer](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/XhE5mPCHdY4/hqdefault.jpg)

Two players in separate places, a walkie-talkie between them. Levers in two
rooms pulled together — guides say count down out loud. **Two levers enter a
coordinate: one moves left and right, the other up and down.** Neither can land
it alone.

[Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Were_Here_(series)) ·

### Space Alert — board game, 2008 (Vlaada Chvátil) — the most useful structure found

![Space Alert, a ten-minute real-time play-through](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/N_ZQTtxTgMw/hqdefault.jpg)

A ten-minute soundtrack is the ship's computer and the clock. It announces
threats as **"Time T plus n: …"** — the threat named *before* it acts. Players
place their actions blind, then everything resolves step by step. Scripted
announcements: *"Unconfirmed report"* (the threat may not be real); *"Incoming
data"* / *"Data transfer"* (a short window in which players may hand each other
cards); *"Communication system down"* (no talking until restored); and phase-end
warnings a minute ahead.

**What survives.** Announce-ahead is the pattern that best survives the voice
delay ([§3.6](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)). The hand-off window
([§3.16](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)) and the unconfirmed report
([§3.17](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)). *Comms down* cannot be
enforced without listening, which rule 5 forbids — see the refusals.

[BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/38453/space-alert) ·
[rulebook](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/c8/67/bd-space-alert-rulebook.pdf) ·
[a real-time play-through](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N_ZQTtxTgMw)

### Captain Sonar — board game, 2016 (Fraga and Lemonnier)

![How to play Captain Sonar](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/U2Diix5qYJo/hqdefault.jpg)

Two submarine crews in real time. After every heading the captain calls, the
engineer marks a breakdown and says **"OK"** — and the captain may not give the
next order until then. The radio operator listens to the *enemy* captain and
deduces where their sub is.

**What survives.** A step that waits for the partner's acknowledgement
([§3.7](#3-boss-sequences-worth-carrying-over)); a noisy trace of the boss's
intent that has to be deduced rather than read.

[BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/171131/captain-sonar) ·
[how to play in eight minutes](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U2Diix5qYJo)

### Artemis, Star Trek: Bridge Crew, PULSAR — spaceship bridges

Every station on its own device, and a captain with no controls who only gives
orders (Artemis); the objectives shown *only* to the captain (Bridge Crew); the
scientist breaching a firewall so the gunner's shots can land (PULSAR — *open
the window, then the other lands the blow*).
[Artemis](https://store.steampowered.com/app/247350/Artemis_Spaceship_Bridge_Simulator/) ·
[Artemis tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ziBAxfFRmAg) ·

### Destiny 2, Deep Stone Crypt — the clearest *one sees, another acts* boss

![How to defeat Taniks](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/mVjAC1g94u8/hqdefault.jpg)

Three roles passed around by picking up buffs: the **Scanner** sees what nobody
else sees, the **Operator** shoots panels, the **Suppressor** stuns the boss.
In the Taniks fight: the Scanner calls which bins glow; the Suppressor stands
under three drones in turn and shoots Taniks from each — **the third hit stuns
him**; *while he is stunned* the carriers deposit their cores in the called
bins; if he traps a carrier in a bubble, the Operator has seconds to shoot it
open. Earlier encounters make the players swap roles at terminals —
"Operator swap!"

[Encounter guide](https://www.redbull.com/gb-en/deep-stone-crypt-encounter-guide-destiny-2-beyond-light) ·

## 3. Boss sequences worth carrying over

Each in this game's words: seats, cannon, shield, hull, lobes, beats, and the
primitives of [the library](bosses-choreographed.md#the-reusable-boss-mechanic-library).
**None of them is a boss yet** — they are steps a boss could be written from.
The verdict column is [the filter](transfers.md#the-filter).

| # | From | The sequence, here | Primitives | Filter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Rhythm Heaven *Shoot-'Em-Up*; Hi-Fi Rush's parry; Great Tiger's jewel | **Shown, then played back.** The boss flashes lobes in an order and a rhythm — *on one phone only*. A bar later it throws that phrase at the hull, and the other phone answers it. The seat who saw the phrase has a bar to say it | `SequenceTap`, `PerSeatTruth`, `DelayedConsequence` | passes all five; the lead is the delay budget |
| 3.2 | Thumper | **Only a clean phrase hurts.** A phrase of steps; a miss anywhere sends the phrase back to its first step and costs nothing else; a clean one lands one hit. Four clean phrases, the boss is down | `StepBack` in its plainest form | passes |
| 3.3 | Sword & Sworcery, Trigon | **A, then B, then both.** Movement one asks only the cannon; movement two only the shield; movement three interleaves them and *the tell says which seat*. The two seats hear the fight as one rhythm with two parts | `BossSequenceStep`'s seat field, `SceneBreak` ×2 | passes; test 4 lives in the third movement |
| 3.4 | Infinity Blade's kicks; Kale's finisher; Minamimoto | **The step whose answer is to wait.** Four wind-ups that look alike; the fourth must *not* be answered, and the detail that says so is drawn on the other phone | `RestraintGate`, `PerSeatTruth` | passes; the one step that punishes the faster player |
| 3.5 | Destiny, Taniks; Spirit Tracks, Malladus; PULSAR | **Three to open it, then the window.** The shield must be up on three marked beats; the third stuns the boss, and only then does the cannon's window open — a few beats, drawn on both phones. A finisher could be a rub | `SimultaneousAction` spread over steps; `AttachedWindow`; rub is new | passes |
| 3.6 | Space Alert | **Announce ahead.** Every step is named N beats before it acts — "four: left lobe" — drawn as a count on both phones, so the call and the delay both fit in the lead | a picture on `BossSequenceStep`; the number is [latency](latency.md)'s | passes; argues for one lead length every boss shares |
| 3.7 | Captain Sonar | **The acknowledge gate.** A step that will not start until the *other* seat taps it clear; the scene resumes on the next beat. The pair sets the pace | `SequentialAction` with the gate held by the other seat | passes; a first-movement tool, since it removes the pressure |
| 3.8 | Operation Tango; Spaceteam | **Crossed panels.** For one movement each phone draws the other's prompt. Both call and act at once | `PerSeatTruth` in both directions | passes; heavy on words, so short phrases only |
| 3.9 | We Were Here Together | **Where and when.** One seat puts the cannon on the column; the other says the beat. Neither lands it alone — worth checking against [roles](roles.md), which may split it this way already | `SplitTerms` | passes |
| 3.10 | Tick Tock's symbol machine | **Symmetric call and press.** Each phone shows one glyph; both must answer both inside a window of N beats. Neither seat is only the caller | `DescribedTarget` both ways | passes |
| 3.11 | Elite Beat Agents; TWEWY's Fusion | **The miss that changes the scene.** A missed step does not end anything: the boss heals, or the scene takes its harder branch | already `BossSequenceStep`'s missed branch | passes; costs content only |
| 3.12 | TWEWY, Draco Cantus; Patapon, Dodonga | **The pose names the gesture.** The boss shows which answer it wants by *how it looks* — a posture only one phone draws truly | `PerSeatTruth`, `DescribedTarget` | passes; the pose must read at 26 px |
| 3.13 | Rock Band | **The unison bar**, and **two saves.** A marked bar judged as a pair: only a clean pair counts. A seat taken out can be brought back twice per boss from a shared meter | `SimultaneousAction` over a bar; a save counter | passes |
| 3.14 | Rhythm Heaven's Remixes; Crakhed | **The final exam.** A last boss made of the earlier bosses' steps, without their tutorials, switching every few bars | none new — content, once the engine exists | passes |
| 3.15 | Magic Touch; Kirby's Paint Panic; Hogwarts duels | **The drawn answer.** A glyph on one phone, described aloud, drawn by the other; a recogniser on the device sends one command | `TraceDrag` or a device-side recogniser; `DescribedTarget` | passes if the recogniser runs on the device |
| 3.16 | Space Alert's data transfer; Overcooked's throw | **The hand-off.** A charge built on one seat's shield is passed to the other seat's cannon on a marked beat; missed, it is lost | `DelayedConsequence`; a seat-to-seat transfer the game lacks | passes |
| 3.17 | Space Alert's unconfirmed report | **The telegraph you cannot trust.** One phone shows a threat; only the other phone can confirm or cancel it | `PerSeatTruth` | passes |
| 3.18 | Sayonara Wild Hearts; Rhythm Doctor 5-X | **Escalate the picture, not the input.** The same step, the thing it answers growing each movement | a look | a look — VERSUS, not here |
| 3.19 | Destiny's *Operator swap!*; Artemis | **The swap.** Which phone sees the tell trades places on a scene boundary, announced a bar ahead | `SceneBreak`, `PerSeatTruth` | passes |
| 3.20 | Destiny's containment bubble; Just Shapes & Beats' revive | **Rescue in time.** One seat is locked; the other has N beats to clear it | `TurnLock` plus a clearing gesture | passes |

**The patterns underneath**, as the three reports found them independently:

1. **Show the phrase, then take it back** — 3.1, and every rhythm game.
2. **One family of answers, with an exception that switches it** — 3.4.
3. **Teach A, teach B, interleave** — 3.3.
4. **Only a perfect chain hurts; a broken chain loops** — 3.2.
5. **The last phrase decides the next** — 3.11.
6. **Escalate by spectacle or metre, not by new inputs** — 3.18.
7. **A final exam from earlier rules** — 3.14.
8. **The game counts, speech says what** — 3.6, and Dodo Re Mi's judging on
   the device. A pattern survives the voice delay exactly when the timing is
   drawn and the voice only carries *which*.

## 4. Every input a phone browser can read

Drawn, one card a gesture with the events it fires, in the director:
DOCUMENTATION › CONTROLS › GESTURES (`tools/director/src/gesture-*.ts`). A
gesture added to either place is added to both.

### 4.1 What the platform offers

As of September 2026. iOS means Safari, including the home-screen app.

| Input | What it gives | iPhone | Android | What bites |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pointer events | press, move, lift, cancel per finger | yes | yes | `touch-action` is read where a touch *starts*; `pointercancel` when the OS takes the gesture |
| Coalesced / predicted samples | the in-between points of a fast move | since Safari 18.2 | yes | without them a fast circle loses samples |
| Touch events | the raw finger list, `force`, contact radius | yes | yes | `force` is dead on iPhone since the iPhone 11 |
| Safari gesture events | two-finger scale and rotation | yes, WebKit only | no | useful mainly to stop page zoom |
| Device motion | accelerometer and gyroscope | **only after `requestPermission()` from a tap** | no prompt | a refusal is silent; desktop claims support and never fires |
| Device orientation | tilt and heading | same gate | yes | tilting fights reading the screen |
| Vibration | a buzz | **no** (a switch-input trick exists, needs a tap) | yes | refused in a background tab |
| Wake lock | the screen stays on | 16.4+, home-screen app 18.4+ | yes | dropped when hidden, must be asked again |
| Fullscreen, orientation lock | hide the browser, lock portrait | **no, on iPhone** — only the installed app | from a tap | — |
| Gamepad, keyboard | buttons | yes | yes | no place for one in a hand on a call |
| Stylus: pressure, tilt, twist | pen data | iPad only | some Samsung | not a phone input |
| Microphone, camera | — | — | — | **the microphone is forbidden (rule 5)**; the camera is out of scope |

Touches that start in the outer ~20 px, the home-indicator strip at the bottom
or the pull-down at the top belong to the OS. An iPhone tracks about five
fingers, Android about ten.

### 4.2 What the game already uses

The plumbing, all in `apps/game/src`: pointer events only (`input.ts`), with
capture, a cancel treated as a lift at no point, the context menu refused, and
every hold released on blur; coalesced samples for bearings (`coalesced.ts`);
`touch-action: none` and no overscroll (`game.css`); the wake lock
(`awake.ts`); a hidden tab treated as a hold (`hidden-hold.ts`); fullscreen and
portrait taken from the READY press (`fullscreen.ts`); two vibrations on
Android (`haptics.ts`); the shake (`shake.ts`); the keyboard at the desk only.
Every in-screen control is in [controls](controls.md).

| Gesture | State | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Tap | built | the panel band, the maw and the shield (`render/touch-ship.ts`), THE BEATBOX, THE MINE, THE DARK |
| Tap versus hold on one control | built | the colour lobes: a tap fires, a hold fills the lance (`sim/lance.ts`) |
| Hold, as a level | built, and the game's commonest verb | `sim/grip.ts`, THE SURGE, THE WARDEN, THE GORGE, THE PULSE and more |
| Timed whole-screen hold | built | the guide and the ready gate (`apps/game/src/briefing.ts`) |

| Swipe, two-way, past a distance | built | the muzzle swipe, THE GUM, THE INSTAR, THE TASTER's wipe |

### 4.3 Missed, worth considering

Each is delay-tolerant: a level held, or a thing graded against a beat window.

- **Rub** — count back-and-forth reversals over a body. The parked LIMPET's
  "move against the grip" ([ideas](ideas.md)) is exactly this, and so is
  Malladus' finisher. Reads as *wipe it clean*.
- **Pinch / squeeze on one body** — two fingers on one target, the separation as
  a depth. A world of blobs asks for it: squeeze a lobe, spread a membrane. Needs
  a one-thumb twin.
- **A drawn glyph, recognised on the device** — one phone shows it, the other
  draws it (3.15). The recogniser runs where milliseconds are allowed and sends
  one command, as the shake does.
- **Chord** — "hold two and five": easy to say, indifferent to the beat, inside
  the iPhone's finger limit at two or three.
- **Double tap**, only where a single tap means nothing — a confirm verb that
  does not slow the fire buttons by the ~250 ms a double tap costs.
- **Tilt as a level**, always with an on-screen twin (THE CHOIR's rule), and
  only after the iOS permission is asked for.
- **Call and response in rhythm** — one phone shows a pattern quantised to the
  beat, the other taps it back. THE BEATBOX's grading already does the judging.
- **Hold, then swipe** (Beatstar) — a held note ending in a direction. Two built
  verbs joined.
- **The iOS motion permission itself** — it turns an input the game already
  has into one that works on half the phones. Queued.

### 4.4 Missed, and should stay missed

- **Pressure.** None on iPhone since 2019, uneven on Android; it would split the
  pair by device.
- **Contact size, pen tilt and twist.** Coarse or absent on a phone.
- **Drags from the physical edge.** The OS owns the edges.
- **Three or more fingers, triple tap, two-finger tap.** They collide with iOS
  accessibility gestures and the finger limit.
- **Flick by speed.** Speed depends on the device, and on the wire a speed is an
  increment — it breaks the rule that a drag sends its whole displacement from
  the grab so a lost move corrects itself. Distance swipes do the job.
- **Two-finger rotate.** One finger round a circle does it better.
- **Face-down.** The player can no longer see their half of the picture.
- **Hold still.** Sensor noise, the permission, and a missing sensor all look
  like stillness. `RestraintGate` is the honest version.
- **Haptics as a signal only one seat gets.** Android only; keep it redundant.
- **The microphone.** Rule 5.

## Refused on the way

- **Anything that dodges, steers or rides** — Infinity Blade's dodge, Thumper's
  track, Sayonara's roads, Just Shapes & Beats' movement, NecroDancer. Test 2.
- **Microphone mechanics** — Spirit Tracks' flute, TWEWY's shouting pins.
  Rule 5.
- **Space Alert's *comms down*.** A game that cannot hear the pair cannot tell
  them to stop talking and notice they did not — test 5. What survives is its
  effect: blank the partner's tell for a bar, so there is nothing to talk about.
- **The Mind's silent phrase.** A phrase where talking does not help is a phrase
  where this game's control scheme is switched off. At most a contrast, rarely.
- **Changing the metre mid-fight** (Rhythm Doctor 1-XN). The beat is the clock
  every window hangs from; a boss in 7/8 asks the beat something it may not
  answer. Not refused, but not a step — a question for [structure](structure.md).
- **Monster Hunter Now's shared view.** Both players see the same thing, so
  there is nothing to say.
