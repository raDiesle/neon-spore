# Bosses, cinematic — nineteen more of the third kind, in words only

> **Status: not built, and not meant to be yet.** The owner asked (18
> September 2026) for a design brief of twenty bosses of the third kind — the
> choreographed scene of `.claude/skills/new-boss` §1, a beat list that will
> not advance until the beat is performed on the boss's own picture — and said
> to implement nothing and touch no code. This page is that brief and nothing
> else: nineteen questions, each with what it splits, what part of the
> silhouette goes away, a beat list naming its gesture, and a self-assessed
> pass against the filters on
> [bosses-choreographed](bosses-choreographed.md#the-filter-these-fifteen-had-to-pass).
> Queued as *Twenty bosses of the third kind: the brief, and where it goes*
> (`docs/queue.md`, answered 19 September 2026): a page of its own beside
> `bosses-choreographed.md`, because that page's fifteen are designed and
> claimed on a ledger and these nineteen are not.
> THE BELLOWS, the fourth of the twenty, was taken out on 24 September 2026
> with the built boss of the same name ([bosses](bosses.md) §11.35).

**Read [bosses-choreographed](bosses-choreographed.md) first.** Its seventeen
concepts, its refused-by-name twelve, its reusable primitive library and its
mapping of the reference brief's own twenty categories are the ground this
page stands on — this brief exists so the space is searched a second time,
by hand, against everything already built rather than against a category
list. The filter section there cites its own no-reskin rule as **filter
8**, and this page uses that same name rather than recomputing the count.

**Self-assessed, not settled.** Filter 8 — *a fourth boss asks a fourth
question* — is the one filter a lane cannot mark for itself with any
confidence: the fifteen already on the other page were themselves picked out
of dozens of cards by the owner's own eye, and several close misses below say
so plainly rather than pretend otherwise. Every entry names its nearest
built neighbour and how it differs; the owner's is the actual verdict, the
same as filter 8 has always been. Nothing here claims to be finished
design — it is nineteen candidates for one afternoon's culling.

**Template**, lighter than the built fifteen's because this is words rather
than a landing: the question, what it splits, the silhouette and its count,
the mechanic in one sentence, a beat list with each beat's gesture named as
the `DragTarget`/`Hold["kind"]` member it is or the primitive it would need
(`bosses-choreographed.md`'s [reusable library](bosses-choreographed.md#the-reusable-boss-mechanic-library)),
THE SLOW or THE DRAG where either applies, and the nearest neighbour.

### 1. THE FERRY — whose aim it is, when only one of you can see where you are pointing

**Splits** hands: the navigator marks, the pilot fires, and neither's screen
draws what the other one is doing to the mark. **Silhouette**: a barge-hulled
body with six mooring cleats along its waterline; each cleat struck while
marked comes free, and the hull rides visibly higher for each one gone.
**Mechanic**: a `drag` from the navigator across the body lays a mark on the
nearest cleat and holds it there only while her thumb stays down; the pilot's
ordinary shot bends to wherever the mark currently sits rather than to his
own crosshair, and his screen never draws the mark at all — only the bend.
**Beats**: (1) `drag` — she picks a cleat and holds; (2) ordinary press,
pilot, bent by the held mark; (3) she re-marks a second cleat as the boss
drifts, still holding the first is refused (one mark, one hand); (4) both
cleats down, the hull's visible list is the payoff frame. **Time**: neither —
the bend is instant, the drama is in the not-seeing. **Nearest neighbour**:
[THE LEDGER](bosses-choreographed.md#5-the-ledger--whose-body-takes-it) turns
the pair's own aim against them after the fact; this turns it aside *during*
the shot, by extending [Marking](couplings.md#2-marking--built-as-the-lance)
from an assist into the whole fight — the closest reuse on this page, flagged
for the owner's eye.

### 2. THE LATCH — whether you can share one grip without ever both letting go at once

**Splits** hands: one clasp per seat on the same hatch, and only one may be
open at a time. **Silhouette**: a hull-hatch body with five ringed latch
plates; each clean handoff pops one plate free. **Mechanic**: each seat holds
a `Hold["latch"]` on their own ring; opening it is refused while the
partner's is already open, and a plate comes free only on the beat one ring
closes inside `latchHandoffBeats` of the other opening — the exact inverse of
[THE SURGE](bosses-choreographed.md#9-the-surge--whether-you-can-stop), which
wants both hands off together. **Beats**: (1) `Hold["latch"]`, pilot; (2)
`Hold["latch"]`, navigator, and the pilot's is refused open again until she
lets hers close; (3) the handoff inside the window pops a plate; (4) missed —
both closed at once, nothing pops, no penalty, try again. **Time**: neither.
**Nearest neighbour**: THE SURGE, named above — same `ReleaseWindow` family,
opposite target (never together instead of always together).

### 3. THE SEXTANT — whether your two guesses average to the truth

**Splits** eyes: both see a drifting point between two rings, neither is told
which ring is theirs to move nor where the point actually is. **Silhouette**:
an armillary frame of four nested rings; each bracket taken burns the
outermost still-whole ring off. **Mechanic**: each seat's `BearingDrag`
(reused from [THE ORRERY](bosses-choreographed.md#2-the-orrery--whether-you-can-agree-on-when))
moves one ring; the point is bracketed, and a ring taken, on the beat the two
rings' *midpoint* lands within `sextantToleranceMilli` of the point — an
arithmetic combination of two positions rather than ORRERY's three
independent cadences. **Beats**: (1–2) both `BearingDrag`, continuous; (3) the
midpoint check runs every beat and silently passes until it lands; (4) a
bracket taken flashes the ring and the point re-seeds further out. **Time**:
SLOW on the final ring's bracket, seen by both. **Nearest neighbour**: THE
ORRERY — same gesture, arithmetic on a sum of two positions rather than three
separate cadences; close enough that the owner may prefer it as ORRERY's
fourth ring instead of a boss of its own.

### 4. THE TALLY — whether you know what they have been doing, not what you have

**Splits** eyes: each seat is shown the *other's* own recent colour spend and
must fire the colour it names. **Silhouette**: a scored tally-stick body,
eight notches; a notch struck in the read colour comes off. **Mechanic**:
reuses `SpendLedger`
([THE TASTER](bosses-choreographed.md#4-the-taster--what-you-have-already-spent)'s
primitive, built), read the other way round — the pilot's panel shows the
navigator's last `TALLY_BEATS` of fire and vice versa, and a shot in the
colour you have *not* been told about does nothing. **Beats**: (1) the tally
turns over, silent, both screens updating at once; (2) ordinary press in the
read colour; (3) a wrong colour is a miss, not a penalty — the notch stands
and the tally keeps turning. **Time**: neither. **Nearest neighbour**: THE
TASTER, named above — same ledger, read across the seat split instead of
along it; the fourth-question test is whether *reading someone else's
history* is different enough from *reading your own* to carry a whole boss,
which is the owner's to weigh.

### 5. THE RATCHET — whether you can catch the same thing twice, in two different places

**Splits** hands: one seat's presses count, and the target moves under them
between presses. **Silhouette**: a cogged drum with seven pawls standing
proud of its rim; each pawl needs three taps and comes off on the third.
**Mechanic**: claims `RepeatedTap` — the one category the second brief asked
for that nothing on the other page wants yet — a press on the live pawl
counts toward its three, and the drum turns one slot the instant a press
lands, so the *next* tap has to find the pawl again rather than repeat a
position. **Beats**: (1) press 1 on pawl at column 4; drum turns; (2) press 2
on pawl now at column 6; drum turns; (3) press 3 on pawl now at column 3, and
it comes free. **Time**: neither — the turn is instant and the whole point is
that it cannot be anticipated by rhythm. **Nearest neighbour**: none
built — the second brief's own table marks repeated-tap absent, and this is
the first concept on either page to spend it.

### 6. THE UNDERWRITE — whether you vouch for a thing you cannot check yourself

**Splits** eyes and hands both: one seat is shown a claim about the boss that
may be true or false, the other is shown nothing and must act on the first
seat's say-so, with a real cost if the claim was wrong. **Silhouette**: a
sealed vault-body with four bands; a band opens when warded on the claimed
side and stays shut, hardened, if the claim was wrong. **Mechanic**: extends
[Announcing](couplings.md#3-announcing--partly-built) — the one coupling
still only partly built — from a single call into a whole fight: the shown
seat sees two candidate bands, one genuine and one a decoy the boss itself
lights convincingly, and must choose which to say; the acting seat's
`Hold["ward"]` on the named band either opens it or hardens it for the round.
**Beats**: (1) two bands light, one seat sees both, the other neither; (2)
the call is spoken, not typed — the game still never evaluates it; (3)
`Hold["ward"]` on the named band; (4) right, it opens; wrong, it hardens and
the next band is offered at a shorter window. **Time**: SLOW on the two bands
lighting, called, per the 900 ms rule. **Nearest neighbour**: none of the
fifteen — Announcing has an assist-level example but no boss built around
being *wrong on purpose sometimes*; closest is
[THE ANTIPHON](bosses-choreographed.md#12-the-antiphon--describing-a-thing-that-has-no-name)'s
candidates, differing because ANTIPHON's rail never lies and this boss's
decoy is drawn to be believed.

### 7. THE FOUNDRY — whether one of you can stop the other's hand in place

**Splits** hands: one seat drags continuously, the other's single timed tap
freezes it exactly where it stands. **Silhouette**: a pouring ladle over a
mould, six mould cells; a cell fills and hardens only while the pour is held
frozen dead centre over it. **Mechanic**: claims `FreezeTap` — card 11 on the
refused sheet, *"the one genuinely new verb"*, named in the library and never
built — one seat's ordinary `drag` walks the ladle along the mould row, the
other's timed tap (a 900 ms called window, since it crosses the voice delay)
freezes the drag in place for `foundryFreezeBeats`; a cell fills only while
frozen over it. **Beats**: (1) `drag`, continuous, walking the ladle; (2)
called: *now* — timed tap freezes it; (3) held frozen, the cell under it
fills; (4) released, the drag resumes from where it was pinned, not from
where it would have drifted to. **Time**: SLOW on the freeze taking, so the
called tap has room inside 900 ms. **Nearest neighbour**: none built — this
is `FreezeTap`'s first home, which is exactly the library's own rule: *a
primitive earns its name on the second boss that needs it, not the first*,
and this would be its first.

### 8. THE FLUME — whether the shape you drew says something to them, later

**Splits** hands then eyes: one seat traces alone, the other reads the result
of the trace rather than following it live. **Silhouette**: a channelled
body, five sluice gates along its base; a gate opens where the traced shape's
*last column* lands. **Mechanic**: a second use of `TraceDrag`
([THE FILAMENT](bosses-choreographed.md#17-the-filament--whether-you-can-follow-a-line-that-is-still-being-drawn)'s
primitive, unbuilt, "wanted by 17, and nothing else" until now) — one seat
draws a free path across the top of the field, a tile a beat, and the *shape*
of it, not the following of it, is what matters: its last column is where the
sluice opens, and the other seat — who never sees the trace being laid, only
the finished channel — has to be told which gate to guard before it does.
**Beats**: (1–4) `drag`, unseen by the partner, laying the path; (5) the path
locks and the last column is revealed as a gate, called across the voice
delay; (6) the other seat wards it. **Time**: SLOW from the path locking to
the gate opening, the one called window. **Nearest neighbour**: THE FILAMENT,
named above — same gesture, and a genuinely different question (a shape as a
*message*, decided once, rather than a line as a *thing followed live*), so
the second use the library asked for is earned rather than borrowed thin.

### 9. THE MANIFOLD — whether you can catch a beat neither of you set

**Splits** hands: two valves, one each, that must both be struck on the same
beat as a third cadence belonging to nobody. **Silhouette**: a triple-piped
manifold, five shared seams; a seam welds shut on a caught beat. **Mechanic**:
the hull's own alarm — already a fixed presentation cue — is repurposed as a
visible pulse on the manifold at an irregular, seeded interval neither seat's
own rhythm predicts; both `Hold["manifold"]` valves released inside one beat
of that pulse welds a seam, unlike
[THE DIASTOLE](bosses-choreographed.md#7-the-diastole--two-clocks-at-once),
where both cadences are the players' own. **Beats**: (1–2) both hold,
waiting; (3) the pulse lights, called and seen at once since both screens
draw it; (4) both release inside the window, or one seam stays open and the
pulse re-seeds. **Time**: SLOW on the pulse itself. **Nearest neighbour**: THE
DIASTOLE, named above — same coincidence-window shape, with the third
cadence taken away from both seats rather than owned by either, which is the
actual new question: catching a beat you did not set, together.

### 10. THE SPOOL — whether you can both keep pulling, not just pull once

**Splits** hands: a body reeling itself in at a fixed rate that only slows
while both are actively dragging against it, continuously. **Silhouette**: a
segmented cable spool, seven segments visible on the drum; each segment held
back a full beat comes loose and drops away. **Mechanic**: unlike
[THE SINEW](bosses-choreographed.md#8-the-sinew--how-hard-not-when)'s
one-shot threshold hold, this wants sustained effort with no slack banked —
both seats' `drag` depth must stay above `spoolMinMilli` for the *whole* beat,
not merely cross it once, or the reel resumes at full rate and the last
segment's progress is lost. **Beats**: (1–2) both drag, sustained; (3) a full
beat held resists the reel and a segment drops; (4) either hand slips below
the floor mid-beat and the segment's progress is lost, not merely paused.
**Time**: DRAG on the reel's own base rate — the whole fight is about buying
beats, not seconds. **Nearest neighbour**: THE SINEW, named above — same
`ChargeSum` machinery, traded for continuous cost instead of a single
crossing, which is closer to a genuinely different question (stamina, not
strength) than most entries here.

### 11. THE OATH — whether you can do nothing, together, at the same time

**Splits** hands: the fight is passed by both thumbs leaving the glass at
once, not by anything pressed. **Silhouette**: a coiled body that unwinds one
turn whenever it is *not* touched; five coils, each stilled turn unwinds one
and it is gone. **Mechanic**: claims `RestraintGate` (unbuilt, "wanted by 3",
[THE GORGE](bosses-choreographed.md#3-the-gorge--what-not-to-do)) a second
time and asks something GORGE does not: GORGE is one seat individually
choosing not to shoot a body; this wants **both** seats' `world.prime`
entirely empty — no drag, no hold, no press, from either phone — for
`oathStillBeats` running, a synchronized stillness rather than an individual
restraint. **Beats**: (1) the coil is live and tightening, ordinary field
controls work as always; (2) called: *now, both of you, hands off*; (3) both
thumbs off the glass, held; (4) either seat touches anything and the count
resets from zero, no penalty beyond the lost beats. **Time**: SLOW while
still, so the called window is survivable. **Nearest neighbour**: THE GORGE,
named above — same primitive, individual restraint versus synchronized
absence; the two are close enough that the owner may want only one boss built
on `RestraintGate`.

### 12. THE OSSUARY — whether the same body means the same thing to both of you, this beat

**Splits** eyes: the same limb reads as the boss's health on one screen and
as its armour on the other, and which is true *changes* every few beats.
**Silhouette**: a jointed carapace, six limbs; a limb taken on the beat it
reads true on both — the only beat it may be shot — comes off. **Mechanic**:
extends `PerSeatTruth` ([the Bulb Queen](bosses.md#110-the-bulb-queen--armoured-everywhere-but-the-mark)'s
primitive, built and "the most-wanted on the page") from a fixed split into a
live, changing one: each limb silently flips which screen reads it as
vulnerable on a seeded clock neither seat is shown, so a shot has to be
called on a limb the caller believes is true and the shooter has to trust it
is true on theirs too, this beat. **Beats**: (1) the flip is silent, unseen
on either screen; (2) a call, across the voice delay; (3) an ordinary press
on the named limb; (4) right on both screens, it comes off; wrong on the
shooter's, nothing happens and nothing is lost. **Time**: neither — the
uncertainty is the whole of it, and slowing it would let the pair simply wait
it out. **Nearest neighbour**: the Bulb Queen, named above — same primitive,
made to disagree with itself over time instead of holding one fixed split,
which is the clearest "same coupling, harder question" entry on this page.

### 13. THE RIVET — whether doing well makes the next beat harder to read

**Splits** hands: every completed beat leaves a mark on the boss's own
surface that partly obscures the next one. **Silhouette**: a riveted plate
body, eight rivets; each driven rivet stays drawn, and the plate is bare only
once all eight are gone. **Mechanic**: an ordinary `press` on the lit rivet
drives it — no new primitive — but the driven rivet's mark is never cleared
from the frame, so by the sixth or seventh the next lit rivet has to be found
among the ones already done; the difficulty curve is drawn by the pair's own
progress rather than by the wave author. **Beats**: (1) press the lit rivet,
it drives and stays drawn, dark; (2) the next rivet lights somewhere among
the driven ones; (3) repeat, the field reading harder each time; (4) the
eighth clears the plate at once. **Time**: neither. **Nearest neighbour**:
none built — several bosses get *harder* as they proceed, but none makes its
own accumulated success the source of the difficulty; closest structurally is
[THE TASTER](bosses-choreographed.md#4-the-taster--what-you-have-already-spent),
which reads the pair's spend rather than the boss's own scar.

### 14. THE SCONCE — whether you can talk them through a beat you cannot see together

**Splits** eyes: one seat's screen goes dark on a schedule the *other's*
does not share, unpredictably and separately, so the lit seat must call a
beat neither of them is looking at together. **Silhouette**: a lantern-hung
body, five sconces; a sconce struck while its seat's screen is dark and the
other's lit comes free. **Mechanic**: a second use of `PerSeatLight`/`Darkness`
([THE CANDLE](bosses-choreographed.md#14-the-candle--whether-you-can-act-in-the-dark)'s
primitive), inverted — CANDLE darkens both screens together on a shared
schedule split by role; this darkens **one screen at a time**, on a seeded
clock the lit seat cannot predict, so the blind seat is not merely acting
without seeing, they are being talked through a picture the other one is
looking at *instead of* sharing it. **Beats**: (1) one screen darkens,
seeded, the other stays lit; (2) the lit seat calls the sconce; (3) the blind
seat's ordinary press, aimed by ear; (4) both screens desync again next beat.
**Time**: neither — a slowed asymmetric blindness would just become a longer
wait. **Nearest neighbour**: THE CANDLE, named above — same primitive, traded
from synchronized to asymmetric, which is close enough that the owner may
prefer it as a CANDLE variant beat rather than a boss of its own.

### 15. THE GANTRY — whether you can keep firing while they are holding the gun down

**Splits** hands: the boss lifts the ship's own cannon mount, and the seat
holding it down cannot fire at all — not merely occupied, actually without a
weapon. **Silhouette**: a boarding-claw body gripping the ship's own gantry,
four grapples; a grapple struck by the free seat's shot, while the held seat
keeps the mount down, tears loose. **Mechanic**: unlike
[The Warden](bosses.md#114-the-warden--the-gate-one-of-you-holds-open)'s
tether, which occupies a spare hand, this claims the firing hand itself: a
`Hold["gantry"]` on the mount refuses that seat's own cannon input for as
long as it is held, so the fight is carried entirely by the free seat's aim
while the held seat's only job is not letting go. **Beats**: (1)
`Hold["gantry"]`, the held seat's cannon goes dark on their own screen; (2)
ordinary press, free seat, at a grapple; (3) repeated at the remaining
grapples; (4) all four gone, the mount releases and both cannons return.
**Time**: neither. **Nearest neighbour**: The Warden, named above — same
"hands free" question, sharpened from an occupied hand to a disabled weapon,
flagged as the closest reuse of a *field*-boss (rather than choreographed)
coupling on this page.

### 16. THE GRIST — whether your misses cost you twice

**Splits** hands: nothing new to aim at, but every shot that hits nothing at
all becomes the boss's next arrival. **Silhouette**: a milling body with
eight sacks along its flank; each sack fed by four of the pair's own misses
bursts and comes off. **Mechanic**: reuses `SpentBody`
([THE SCUTTLE](bosses-choreographed.md#15-the-scuttle--a-boss-racing-you-to-its-own-death)'s
primitive, built) fed the opposite way — SCUTTLE throws its own frame apart
regardless of the pair's accuracy; this counts every shot that connects with
neither a creature nor the boss and spawns a rock at the wave's ordinary
arrival rate for every `gristMissCount` such misses, so carelessness during
an ordinary wave earlier in the run is what fills this boss's sacks later.
**Beats**: nothing scripted beat-to-beat — the whole fight is the accumulated
tally arriving as ordinary rock pressure, cleared by ordinary shots at the
sacks between waves of it. **Time**: neither. **Nearest neighbour**: THE
SCUTTLE, named above, and also
[THE LEDGER](bosses-choreographed.md#5-the-ledger--whose-body-takes-it) in
spirit — a cost that travels backward in time from the pair's own play — this
is closer to a *wave-design* idea than a scene, and the owner may want it
built as a difficulty modifier rather than a boss.

### 17. THE WICKER — whether you remember what you started

**Splits** hands: one seat plants a mark now, and its consequence arrives
several beats later at a place both of them have to remember to clear.
**Silhouette**: a woven, spreading body, six knots; a knot cleared before its
consequence lands comes free. **Mechanic**: claims `DelayedConsequence`
(unbuilt, "the brief's central diagram," wanted by 5 and 13) — a `drag` plants
a mark on a knot, and `wickerDelayBeats` later the column under that knot
opens a breach unless it has been warded by then; nothing is drawn to remind
either seat which column is coming, so the debt is carried in memory rather
than on the panel. **Beats**: (1) `drag`, plants the mark, the knot glows
briefly and fades; (2–N) ordinary field play, nothing marks the clock
running; (N+1) called, if remembered: *ward column four*; (N+1) unremembered:
the breach opens and the knot hardens rather than clearing. **Time**: neither
— the whole point is that no window is drawn to slow down for. **Nearest
neighbour**: [THE MIRROR](bosses.md#113-the-mirror--your-own-ship-asking-for-your-moves-back)
asks for the pair's remembered moves read back to them by the boss; this asks
them to remember a debt nothing ever reads back, which is the sharper
version and the reason `DelayedConsequence` is still unbuilt.

### 18. THE QUARRY — whether you can keep a shared sense of whose turn it feels like

**Splits** hands: unlike a fixed alternation, this fight tracks who acted
*least recently* and asks for them next, without ever saying so. **Silhouette**:
a stepped quarry-face body, seven ledges; a ledge struck by whichever seat is
due comes free, struck by the other does nothing. **Mechanic**: unlike
`Alternation` ([THE BATON](bosses-choreographed.md#10-the-baton--whose-turn-is-it),
built, a hard refusal of a repeat), this keeps a rolling **soft** turn — the
seat who has fired least in the last `quarrySpanBeats` is the one whose shots
count — read by neither seat's screen, so the pair has to feel out loud
whose turn it is rather than read it off a lock. **Beats**: (1) either
presses; if due, the ledge cracks; (2) the other presses next, likely now
due; (3) a guess wrong costs nothing but the shot, so a pair that loses track
merely stalls rather than fails. **Time**: neither. **Nearest neighbour**:
THE BATON, named above, and its closest twin on this page — the owner may
reasonably fold this into a BATON variant rather than a boss of its own,
since the difference (soft tracking versus a hard lock) is a tuning knob more
than a new question.

### 19. THE ZENITH — the finale that spends everything already built

**Splits** whichever each of its scenes already splits — this is the "final
multi-mechanic cinematic" category
[bosses-choreographed](bosses-choreographed.md#the-twenty-categories-and-where-each-already-is)
names and leaves "named, unwritten," and it asks no new question of its own
on purpose: filter 8 does not apply to a finale built entirely out of moves
the pair has already learned. **Silhouette**: no single body — a sequence of
three `SceneBreak`-separated movements (unbuilt, the second brief's own scene
layer), each reusing one already-shipped scene's silhouette at a smaller
scale, so what comes apart is the sum of what the whole page has already
built. **Mechanic**: authored entirely from shipped primitives —
`PerSeatTruth`, `Slow`, `SimultaneousAction`, `Alternation` — stitched by
`BossSequenceStep` into one closing beat list with no new gesture and no new
hashed field. **Beats**: a movement each drawn from THE INSTAR's cursor
mechanism, THE ANTIPHON's description split and THE BATON's alternation, in
that order, ending on a `MutualRelease` (unbuilt, wanted by THE SURGE) shared
by both seats at once. **Time**: SLOW on the very last beat, both screens,
the only truly shared slow window on this page. **Nearest neighbour**: none —
it is explicitly not a fourth question, it is the page closing on the third
kind's own claim that a scene can be *authored* rather than built, the same
sentence [bosses-choreographed](bosses-choreographed.md#the-briefs-own-worked-encounter-read-against-this-engine)
already made about `BossSequenceStep`. Wants `MutualRelease`, `SceneBreak` and
`StepBack` before it can be written at all — the last of the four
build-first primitives this brief never needed on its own, and the one
`THE ZENITH` needs from all of them at once.

## What this brief is not

there is nothing to collide over yet — the claim happens the ordinary way,
`.claude/skills/new-boss` §3, the day the owner picks one to build.

**Six of the nineteen are flagged as close to a shipped neighbour** in their

comes to the brief's own "final multi-mechanic" ask being answered by
spending debt rather than inventing more of it.
