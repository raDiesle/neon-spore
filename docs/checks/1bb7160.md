## `1bb7160` — the falling shadow's own stages, side by side

> on the STATES page, do the three stages of a falling shadow show it gathering under the rock — and now that you can see it side by side, does it read in play?

- **badge** implementation
- **subject** GAME MECHANICS' STATES tab (`tools/director/src/states-page.ts`), a new FALLING SHADOW row
- **changed** three real frames of one rock, built straight off `pose-kit.ts`'s own exports since `poses-mechanics.ts` sits outside this lane's paths — entering the lead window, the window's own midpoint, and the last tick the rock exists before `resolveHull` removes it
- **decide** does the row show the shadow gathering under the rock as it falls, and does that same gathering read in play at tempo?
- **before** nothing — the STATES page had no way to hold a falling shadow still at all, so there is no prior picture to contrast it with
- **after** `docs/checks/1bb7160-after.png` — one rock, three ticks, labelled ENTERING / HALFWAY / AT CONTACT
- **where** director → GAME MECHANICS → STATES → FALLING SHADOW

**What the picture actually shows.** The three frames are not evenly legible: ENTERING and HALFWAY read as almost the same picture — no visible darkening under the rock in either — and only AT CONTACT shows a clear dark patch filling the hull's own lobe dip. That is one finding, not two: the shadow's alpha ramps too gently across the range it can actually reach to read as "gathering" in a still frame, and the range it can reach is itself short of the effect's own design. `contact-shadow.ts`'s `t` is meant to hit `1` — its darkest, tightest, most centred state — at the instant of contact, but `resolveHull` (`packages/sim/src/hull.ts`) removes a falling body the same tick its row would reach the hull, before that beat's own interpolation ever draws a frame there. For this config the last drawn frame stops at `t ≈ 0.667`, one row short, for every creature kind — not only meteors. `contact-shadow.ts` is left unchanged: the fix for the never-reached endpoint lives in `hull.ts`'s removal timing, outside this lane's paths, and the fix for the gentle-in-range ramp is a curve decision the picture raises but does not, on its own, resolve — a front-loaded alpha would need the owner's own eye on it, the same as any other look.
