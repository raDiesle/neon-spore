# Alive

Why the field reads as drawn rather than living, and what is being done about
it. Three readers diagnosed the current state, three directions were designed
from different first principles — motion first, surface first, behaviour
first — and three judges scored them on whether it survives 26 px, whether the
pair can still name what they see, and whether the machine survives it.

The constraint that outranks beauty, and that killed several otherwise good
ideas: **a silhouette must mean the same word every time.** Two people say
shapes out loud across a two-second delay. A body that is more alive and less
nameable is a net loss.

## The direction

**THE FLINCH, with the interior grafted on last.** Take Direction 3 as the spine: the six contour numbers in `silhouettes.ts` are frozen against every reaction, and everything that lives goes into two channels that cannot cost a name — bounded pose impulses that decay back to the canonical shape, and light under the skin. The pose's clock moves off `view.time` (which `apps/game/src/main.ts` fills from `performance.now()`, so today the two phones sway the same creature differently) and onto `world.beat + beatPhase`, which is lockstep state; per-body phase moves off `(c.id % 7) * 0.9` — seven phases on an eleven-column field, so neighbours in lockstep are routine — onto a continuous integer hash. On that clock the body gets a landing squash and a pre-beat gather in unison, because every creature moves one row on the same instant and pretending otherwise would lie about the game's only rule, while the sway, the drift and the light stay offset per id so the field is in time without being in step. Reactions are then hung off information render already holds and the sim already owns: `c.row` against `hullRow` (dread, and a doubled gather in the last row before impact), `gripsCreature` (strain), `Effects.blocked` (a 60 ms hit-stop drawn lerped fully to `REST`, *then* a recoil), and the `destroy`/`runtHit`/`petal` events `Effects.ingest` already receives (a neighbourhood flinch). Three things from the losing directions are grafted because they are measurably right: the bulb's `depth` is deepened before the freeze applies — it is the one edit in the whole exercise that makes a creature *easier* to name, since BULB and THROB both sit at aspect ~1.0 and are separated only by lobe count and area; `strokeGlow`'s `color` parameter is widened to `string | CanvasGradient` under a colour-only-never-alpha rule, which varies apparent line weight around the loop for one word and zero extra draw calls; and a cached radial `coreFill` replaces the flat `dark` swatch, whose outermost stop mixes toward *background* so it raises rim-to-interior contrast rather than eroding it. Cut, permanently: the travelling gleam (a 9 px additive dot on a 30 px body reads as a bullet), the second organ, all runt interior work (the runt draws at ~10 px, below graphics.md's own "at 11 px nothing of a figure survives" line), and every proposal to drift, unmirror or breathe the detail dots, which are 1.0 px in radius. The whole batch is gated by one new build check before any amplitude exists.

## Why this one, what was grafted, what was refused

**Why D3.** Two of three judges picked it and the third grafted its three best items onto D1. It is the only direction that spends where the pixels are: shock recoil is up to 0.35 tiles ≈ 12 px of whole-body translation at the most-watched instant in the game, against D1's headline four-beat breath whose visible half is ±3.5% scale — 1 px — and D2's flagship contour breath, which is arithmetically wrong (`m *= 1 + 0.045*sin` is multiplicative on r ≈ 14 px at 11 cols on a 390-wide phone, so 0.64 px, not the "~1.2 px and reads" it claims). It is also the only direction that budgets *brightness* rather than only cost, and "creatures stay the brightest thing on the field" is a ratio: D1 and D2 both add light on both sides of the rim and subtract none. And its ordering is right — the interior is item 8 of 11, explicitly droppable.

**What was grafted, and why each survived.** (a) `BULB.depth` 0.1 → 0.13, from D2. D3's structural freeze is its best feature and its one blind spot; nine lobes at depth 0.1 downsamples to a circle, and bulb/throb is the pair a voice callout most needs separated. Landed as a first commit, before the freeze is declared. (b) `strokeGlow(color: string | CanvasGradient)`, from D2 — one word in a signature, every existing caller unaffected, and it buys what D3's gleam was reaching for at a fraction of the risk. D2's discipline comes with it: colour-only stops, never alpha, enforced by named deep swatches in `palette.ts`, because a gradient that reaches zero alpha opens a hole in the outline and a silhouette with a missing bottom edge is a different word. (c) D1's hit-stop, in front of D3's jolt rather than instead of it. D3 answers a blocked shot with *more* motion at the moment the player is staring hardest at that one body; D1's version holds the canonical silhouette dead still for 60 ms first, and it is the only item anywhere in the exercise that increases legibility instead of merely not harming it. (d) D1's `coreFill` and its cache discipline — a `Map` keyed by colour and shape, at most twelve gradient objects for the life of the process, and any halo radius quantised, which is the guard `sheen.ts` already documents with `Math.round(…/4)*4` and which `haloSprite`'s `${color}@${radius}` key genuinely needs.

**Four corrections the judges found and the lanes must carry, or the batch fails.** *One:* `poseAt`'s argument changes meaning from seconds to beats with no type change across 14 tool call sites and ~12 draft motions in `tools/shape-sheet/src/motions.ts` written in seconds — the sheet would then judge a motion the game does not have, at 1.6× the wrong rate, with a green typecheck. That is exactly the failure `own-motion.ts`'s own header says it was extracted from `render/creatures.ts` to prevent, running in reverse. It lands as a branded `Beats` type so every call site fails loudly. *Two:* `packages/sim/test/purity.test.ts` line 168 asserts `own-motion.ts` still contains `t * 1.9` or `t * 1.35`; the beat rewrite deletes both and turns `bun run check` red on the first commit. The row moves in the same commit. *Three:* D3's aspect-ratio gate is unsatisfiable as written — SLICK measures 146.7/87.0 ≈ 1.69 and BULB, THROB and RUNT all sit at ~1.0, so three ranges already overlap before a line is written, and the gate either fails on the current build or quietly degrades to slick-versus-everything, which is the half that was never in danger. It needs two more axes and an "all axes at once" rule. *Four:* nobody in three directions noticed that `drawLiving` composes `ctx.scale(scale * sx, scale * sy)`, so a non-uniform pose strokes the outline with an elliptical pen — line weight varies by direction at exactly the instant the squash peaks. This exists today at SWAY_PUMP's ±10%; D3's landing takes it to 18–22%, a 20% swing in a stroke `docs/spec/graphics.md` pins at 1.2–1.8 px, and D2's rim gradient is proposed specifically to vary apparent line weight while this varies it unintentionally in the wrong place.

**Rejected, with grounds.** D3's derived rotation: its claim that constants can be "chosen to reproduce today's look exactly" is false — a finite difference of `sin(t*1.35)` is a quarter-cycle lag and the authored offset is 0.5 rad — and deleting `rot` from the four `poseAt` bodies leaves `tools/director/src/scene-art.ts:153` and three call sites in `shapes-motion.ts` drawing bodies that never rotate. It buys nothing visually by design, so it goes to a vote rather than into a lane. D2's beat-in-the-contour: it puts the pulse in the word, threads a lockstep quantity through `blobRadiusMul` — reached by `shapes.ts`, `queen-glyph.ts`, `pods.ts`, `controls.ts`, three shape-sheet forms and `apps/game/src/menu-view.ts`, which has no world and no beat to give it — and phases only by the per-kind `seed`, so a single-kind wave inflates in perfect unison. D1's unclipped organ light: it is defended on cost and never on the read; a soft same-colour bloom just outside the outline is the brief's third named failure verbatim. D1's `BULB.wobble` 0.055 → 0.075: it lands on the one body whose lobe count is already marginal, where a time-varying 3rd/5th harmonic at comparable magnitude to `depth` makes the lobe count ambiguous *between frames*. D1 argues the slick case carefully and states the bulb number without argument.

## Free wins — a different number in a parameter set

Six changes that are a different number in an existing parameter set. Every one is A/B-able the moment it is typed, three of them with `bun run shapes:report` alone and no picture at all.

**In `packages/content/src/silhouettes.ts` (lane 1 — these land first, before the contour freeze is declared):**

- `BULB.depth`: **0.1 → 0.13**. Ceiling 0.15, past which nine lobes read as a gear. The highest-value number on this list and the only proposal in the whole exercise that makes a creature easier to name rather than merely not harder: the report measures BULB at 117.3 × 111.7 and THROB at 102.5 × 103.2 — two round bodies at aspect ~1.0, separated today only by area and by `throbOpen`'s swell — and at 9 lobes on a ~32 px body, depth 0.13 gives roughly 2.1 px of lobe amplitude at ~11 px spacing, which resolves at DPR 3.
- `SLICK.wobble`: **0.02 → 0.045**. Ceiling 0.07, past which the 5th harmonic starts reading as extra lobes. The slick is the flagship body and currently has the least contour life of anything living — measured TRAVEL 7.0 shape units, about 1.46 px on screen, so its edge moves less than a pixel. `depth: 0.38` carries the two-lobe read entirely, so a ±4.5% radius modulation cannot collapse it; the report's lobe extent must be unchanged before and after.
- `RUNT.wobble`: **0.02 → 0.03**. Small on purpose: the runt draws at ~10 px and has no pixels to spare.
- **Not** `BULB.wobble` 0.055 → 0.075, which D1 proposed. See the rationale: it is the one body whose lobe count is already marginal.

**In `packages/content/src/own-motion.ts` (lane 3, in the same commit as the beat conversion):**

- `TREMBLE`'s frequency triple: **8.7 / 13.1 / 5.3 → 8.3 / 12.7 / 5.9**. Further from any multiple of the 1.6 Hz beat, so the one creature the pair must not shoot keeps reading as debris caught in the wave rather than as part of it. This is the only number in `own-motion.ts` this batch changes that is not a unit conversion.

**In `packages/render/src/creatures.ts` (lane 4, the lift — banked as headroom, not spent):**

- `drawMotionTrail`'s loop bound: **`k <= 4` → `k <= 2`**, spacing `l.tile * 0.3 → 0.26`. Forty halo blits a frame on a ten-body field, for an effect the spec never asks for and which smears each body's brightness up its own column — actively against "creatures stay the brightest thing on the field". Two still reads as direction.
- `blobPath`'s `N` argument: **default 40 → pass 28 at the creature call site only**, leaving `tools/shape-sheet` at the 40 default. The parameter already exists. At r ≈ 14 px a 40-point Catmull-Rom is about two control points per screen pixel of arc. Honest accounting: this cuts string construction and `Path2D` parsing, which is a different bill from rasterisation and compositing, so it does **not** pay for added fills, clips or `lighter` passes — bank it rather than counting it against them.

## What goes to a vote rather than to a lane

See `docs/versus.md` for the mechanism.

The VERSUS mechanism is already queued (`claude/burn-versus-v1` through `-v5` in `docs/queue.md`) and its slot lane deliberately goes last, after somebody has watched the pair run. Five slots, in this order — and note the standing constraint, restated: **no slot may patch `SWAY_PUMP` or `TILT_RIPPLE` until lane 3 has landed.** `claude/burn-own-motion-b10` is already on `main`, but lane 3 rewrites both records onto beats, and a vote taken against a record that is about to move is a vote against nothing.

**1. `creature:bulb` — how deep is a lobe.** `depth` at 0.10 (current), 0.13 (what lane 1 lands by argument), and the queue's own worked example, 6 lobes at 0.19. This is the highest-value vocabulary question in the game and the argument only gets it as far as "deeper than 0.10"; where exactly it sits between 0.13 and 0.19 is an eye at 26 px beside a throb, which is precisely what the pair of phones exists for. Vote it first because lane 1's number is the one thing in the batch that a later vote can cleanly overturn — it is three characters in `silhouettes.ts` plus `bun run shapes`.

**2. `creature:landing` — is unison tempo, or twelve metronomes.** Full `landGain` (bulb 1.0 / slick 0.6 / runt 0.4 / throb 0) against every gain halved with the directional gather left at full. This is the single largest risk in the batch and it cannot be settled by argument at all: every body squashing on every downbeat is either the shared clock made visible or the exact opposite of alive, and the difference is only findable at tempo on a phone. It is also the perfect BLINK candidate — the two sides differ in one scalar, so side-by-side will show almost nothing and the alternating overlay will show everything.

**3. `creature:slick` — authored lag against derived lag.** `TILT_RIPPLE` as it stands (rotation offset half a radian behind its own drift) against rotation derived from a finite difference of that drift. This lane refuses to land the derived version because D3's claim that it reproduces today's look is measurably false — a finite difference gives a quarter-cycle, about 1.57 rad, against the authored 0.5 — but "measurably different" is not "worse", and the derived version is what would make lane 6's shock recoil read as physics rather than as a nudge, because a slick shoved sideways would tumble and a bulb would swing without either case being written. That is a real payoff behind a real look change, which is a vote. Bundle `SLICK.wobble` 0.02 / 0.045 / 0.07 into the same slot, since a tilt and an edge wobble are not judged apart.

**4. `creature:interior` — is the spec right that detail does not survive.** The flat `dark` swatch against lane 8's `coreFill` plus the clipped membrane stroke. `docs/spec/graphics.md` says liveliness at this size comes from motion, not detail; lane 8's premise is that a value gradient is not detail. One of those is wrong and no amount of arguing settles it, so build the lane, then vote it — and if the flat swatch wins, lane 8 is a `git revert` and the batch loses nothing that was carrying weight.

**5. `creature:trail` — two halos or four.** The cheapest slot here and worth having because it is the one place the batch spends someone else's brightness: the trail smears each body's light up its own column, which works directly against "creatures are the brightest thing on the field", and the only question is whether two blits still say "this moved". A five-second vote.

Not a slot, deliberately: the shock recoil. Whether a neighbour's flinch reads as sympathy or as damage is not a comparison between two looks, it is a question about what a player *assumes* and then does — it needs somebody firing into a cluster with a partner on the other phone, and a `Check:` trailer is the right instrument, not a vote.

## Open questions

Six things this plan does not decide, separated into what goes to a vote and what needs the owner's own judgement.

**Taste, and therefore votes (slots 1-5 above).** Bulb lobe depth beyond "deeper than 0.10". Whether the unison landing reads as the shared clock or as a field of metronomes — I have written the fallback into lane 5 rather than pretending the hedges are evidence. Whether the slick's tilt should keep its authored lag or take a derived one that changes its look. Whether an interior gradient survives 26 px at all, which is a straight disagreement with the spec's own sentence and which lane 8's premise may simply lose. And two trail halos against four.

**Not taste, and not mine to decide.** *The runt is drawn at about 10.3 px* — 39.6 shape units at `sizeMul: 0.55` against r ≈ 14 px on a 390-wide phone with 11 columns. That is below `docs/spec/graphics.md`'s own line that at 11 px nothing of a figure survives, and all three design proposals prescribed runt-specific interior work that cannot exist there. This batch deletes every one of those items, which is the correct local answer and dodges the real question: either the runt gets nothing but tremble amplitude and the absence of rhythm forever, or `sizeMul` goes up. Raising it is not a rendering change — a runt that reads as a small slick is a creature the pair shoots by mistake, which is the whole mechanic — so it is the owner's call and it belongs in `docs/parked.md` if it is not taken now.

*Three of the four living kinds are aspect ~1.0.* This is the thing no direction and no judge would own, and it is probably the highest-value nameability work in the codebase. BULB, THROB and RUNT are separated only by lobe count (9, 6, 4) and by size, and lobe counts of that fineness are the first thing to die on a downsampled 26 px body. Everything in this batch adds light and reaction to a roster whose *silhouettes* are already crowded, and interior light is exactly what does not survive downsampling. Lane 1 deepens the bulb, which is one edit against a structural problem. Pulling the throb and the runt apart in *shape* rather than only in size may be worth more than the other seven lanes combined — and it is a content decision with gameplay in it, not a render pass, so nobody here should make it.

*Whether the shock recoil lies about the rules.* Lane 6 ships with the mitigations and a `Check:` trailer. If a player fires again because three bodies flinched, the item is worse than nothing and comes straight out; that is a playtest with two people, not a comparison of two pictures.

*Nothing in this plan has been measured on a phone.* The claim that `blobPath`'s string build and `Path2D` parse dominate the per-creature cost is plausible from reading `shapes.ts` — 40 points, five trig calls each, 240 `toFixed`, a ~2 KB string re-parsed — but the four strokes of a 40-segment bezier from `strokeGlow` (`STROKE.glowPasses` is 3, plus the solid stroke) are a serious rasterisation cost that no direction accounted for and that the N cut does not touch. If the batch lands and the frame rate moves, the cut order is: lane 8's membrane stroke, then lane 8 entirely, then lane 6's shock, then lane 5's landing on the meteor.
