BULB QUEEN's flanking torches were drawn with nothing holding them — a bare rock in the flank column, and the picture never said why it stayed there. Three drafts answered that, spread on one axis: how much of the holder is machine and how much is her. CRADLE won — her own body doing the holding, and nothing else — and the other two stay below as the record of what it was chosen against. None of this is wired into the game; the shipped queen is unchanged. All three run on one clock, holding for three beats and letting go on the fourth, because how each one lets go is half the question.

THREE WHOLE-BODY DRAFTS, CRADLE AT BOTH FLANKS

Keeping the holder question settled, these three answer a second one: how much of her condition the body itself admits, before the petal row or the sink confirm it. HAIRLINE says all of it, permanently, in the shell. UNDERGLOW says it as light without touching the outline. WITHDRAWAL says it as posture alone. Every card keeps the same two marks with only one real, the same columns either side of her, the same torch-is-the-rock sockets and the same regrowing replacement — only her outline, her material and how she carries a hit are open. All six cards above and below share one clock.

Read off two other co-op games — Hazelight's It Takes Two and Split Fiction — and mapped, row by row, onto whether it can reach this one. Most of it cannot, and the reason is always the same: their unit of play is a character crossing a space and ours is a column. The verdict column is the point, and a NO with its reason in it is worth as much here as a yes.

The same treatment, turned on 2D tower defence — Galaxy Defense: Fortress TD, Bloons, Plants vs Zombies, Kingdom Rush and Asteroids — and asking one question: what else can a slick, a bulb or a meteor be, without becoming a fourth creature. Their geometry is ours, so almost everything transfers and the work is saying which of it we already have under another name. The pictures are linked from those games' own wikis and stores, never copied in: none of that art is ours and this repository is public.

The same treatment, turned on the tool doing the building: if the subscription paying for the agent on this project changed from Anthropic's to OpenAI's, which of the tasks actually done here get better and which get worse. Task by task, with a verdict column, and honest about what it is not — nothing in it was measured on this repository, the way delegation was, and the last section says what one lane built twice would have to record to settle it.
